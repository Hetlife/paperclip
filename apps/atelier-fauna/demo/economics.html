The numbers

The category is real. The pricing wasn’t.

Every figure on this page has a source, and every assumption is labelled as one. That distinction matters here, because researching the original plan’s price points is what produced its central finding.

Headline

The market is genuine — two billion-dollar US categories, millions of households, reptiles the fastest-growing pet segment. But the prices in the original plan sat 3–8× above what these species actually retail for. At real prices, selling the animals alone does not cover paid customer acquisition on a first order. The business case rests on what surrounds the animal, not the animal.

Finding 1

Assumed prices vs. what these species actually sell for

Retail asking prices from active US sellers, against the figures in the original plan. Same species, same market, same week.

A luxury brand can price above market — but it has to know what market is, and be able to say what the premium buys. The plan’s figures weren’t a premium over a known baseline; there was no baseline.

Finding 2

What’s left after acquiring the customer

Gross margin on one animal at researched prices, less the cost of acquiring the buyer. Luxury e-commerce CAC averages $175 (range $120–400).

Repeat purchase

Luxury has the best CAC-to-LTV ratio in e-commerce at 5.2×. But an emerald tree boa is a 15–20 year commitment — this category has structurally low repeat rates on the animal itself.

Cheaper acquisition

Content and organic search instead of paid. Slow, but this category has real editorial substance to work with — which is what the Sanctuary Journal already is. Note CAC rose 40–60% between 2023 and 2025.

Sell more than the animal

Enclosure, planting, installation, ongoing maintenance. Larger order value, recurring revenue, no mortality risk on the hardware. This is the one that changes the arithmetic rather than hoping around it.

Finding 3

One species had to be pulled

Golden Mantella Mantella aurantiaca

Listed as available on waitlist. Research found it is IUCN Critically Endangered with an area of occupancy under 10 km², CITES Appendix II with an annual export quota in the low hundreds, and export suspended since 2010 pending non-detriment findings. Overcollection for the pet trade is a named driver of its decline.

Offering it would have contradicted the custodianship charter the whole site is built on. It is now display-and-education only, and blocked at the API so it cannot be requested.

This is the concrete argument for researching a species before listing it rather than after. One of five had a disqualifying legal status that nobody had checked.

The read

The animal is the cheapest thing being sold

A specimen researches at $85–495. An engaged hobbyist spends ~$1,500 a year, and the reptile market counts enclosures and supplies inside its $1.2–1.5B. A living biotope — custom enclosure, planting, installation, ongoing maintenance — carries a materially higher order value, generates recurring revenue, has no live-animal mortality risk on the hardware portion, and sits directly on an architecture and design background.

Worth noting: this is the same conclusion the ornamental fish export research reached independently, where a habitat design studio was parked for re-examination. Two separate lines of research now point the same way. Treat the animal as the entry point to a biotope commission, not as the product.

Honest gaps

What we still don’t know

None of the below was researched. None should be presented to anyone as known.

  • Wholesale cost of any species. The 40% margin above is an assumption. Real margin needs supplier quotes.
  • Mortality and DOA rates on the actual shipping routes.
  • State-level legality per species, per destination. US live-animal law is state-level; federal screening alone is not sufficient to ship.
  • Whether a premium over market clears. No evidence yet that buyers here pay above the researched band for presentation and documentation.
  • Real conversion rate for this offer. The 1.17% DTC median is across all e-commerce; a consultation-gated flow with no cart converts very differently — possibly much better, possibly far worse.
  • Biotope commission pricing. The recommendation above is a direction, not a costed model.
