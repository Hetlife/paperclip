## 2. Business Model

**Model type**
[DECIDED] B2B export trading, evolving from brokerage to principal.

Two structures, and the choice matters more than anything else here:
- **(a) Broker** — never take title, take 5–15% commission on FOB value. Low capital, low risk. But 10% is roughly half the Indian exporter's own net margin, so both sides are motivated to cut us out by shipment four. Margin decays toward zero.
- **(b) Exporter of record** — register with MPEDA under the "Ornamental Fish Exporter (OF)" category, invoice the US buyer directly, own the customer relationship. Gross margin 25–40% instead of 10%. Requires working capital, carries mortality risk and compliance liability.

**Recommendation:** start as (a) for the first 2–3 shipments to learn the mechanics with minimal exposure, then move to (b) before the relationship gets disintermediated. Treat brokerage as a training phase, not the business.

**What exactly we sell**
[DECIDED] Captive-bred freshwater ornamental fish and aquatic plants only, in v1.

Explicitly excluded on legal grounds: marine species (11 species plus all seahorses/pipefish are Schedule 1 protected under the Wild Life Protection Act — trade prohibited), any CITES/Schedule IV listed species including Indian tarantulas (Poecilotheria), reptiles, and any wild-caught stock.

**Pricing**
[UNDECIDED] Cannot be set before we know real FOB prices from Indian exporters and real landed prices US buyers pay today. This requires actual quotes, not estimates.

**Cost to deliver**
Freight (air, live cargo), packing and oxygen, health certificate, customs clearance both ends, payment processing/FX, and DOA replacement provision. Industry-normal mortality on live shipments is 5–20% — this must be priced in, not treated as an exception.
[UNDECIDED] Actual rupee figures. Come from shipment one.

**Revenue goals**
- Month 1: ₹0. The goal is one completed shipment, not revenue.
- Month 6: ₹40k–80k/month gross margin (2–3 shipments/month at brokerage rates).
- Year 1: ₹1L–1.5L/month, achievable only if we have converted to exporter-of-record and hold 4+ repeat buyers.

Reality check: at 10% commission on $5,000 shipments, ₹1L/month gross needs 2–3 shipments monthly. India's entire national ornamental fish export trade was reported at roughly USD 1.43M in 2017. This is a small pond — the ceiling on a pure brokerage play is low.

## 3. Validation

**Has anyone paid**
[DECIDED] No. Nobody has paid for anything. There is no validation of any kind yet.

**Cheapest, fastest test**
[DECIDED] One shipment, end to end, done manually — spreadsheet and phone, no software, no website, no AI agents. One US buyer, one MPEDA-registered exporter, one consignment of captive-bred freshwater fish.

What it reveals: real DOA rate, real freight cost, real customs friction, real margin, and — most importantly — whether a US buyer will actually wire money to a stranger in Surat. This single shipment produces better information than any amount of further planning.

Before that, a cheaper pre-test: contact 20 US wholesalers/retailers, ask what they currently import from South Asia and what would make them try a new Indian source. Cost: time only. If 20 conversations produce zero interest, stop here.

**What would kill this idea**
- No US buyer will pay a first-time Indian supplier without an escrow or LC that erodes the margin
- Landed cost cannot beat Sri Lanka / Thailand / Singapore at equivalent quality
- DOA rates on our route run above ~15% consistently
- The Indian exporters we can access won't hold a 10% commission structure
- Legal review finds the species we can actually source profitably are all restricted
- Founder time drops below what a live-animal logistics business needs (this is not a passive business)

## 4. Legal & Operations Basics

**Entity**
[DECIDED — see decisions.md] Separate entity from SevaaConnect Solutions Pvt Ltd. A compliance incident in live-animal export should not touch the architecture practice's name or balance sheet.

**Country / tax**
India (Gujarat) for registration and taxes. US-side obligations depend on whether we ship on DDP or FOB terms.

**Licences and registrations required — verify each with counsel before quoting anyone:**
- IEC (Import Export Code) from DGFT
- MPEDA registration under the Ornamental Fish Exporter (OF) category, valid three years — note OF registrants may not export live marine products for human consumption
- Health certificate per destination-country requirement
- US side: the Lacey Act makes it a federal offence to import wildlife taken, possessed or sold in violation of foreign law. A commission-taking intermediary sits inside that liability chain. Any competent US importer will demand the exporter's documentation before paying.

**Contracts / ToS / privacy**
Yes — supplier agreement, buyer terms covering DOA policy and payment terms, and a privacy policy if we collect enquiries through a website. DOA policy is the single most negotiated clause in this trade; get it right first.

**Bookkeeping**
[UNDECIDED] Founder preference. Existing CA relationship through SevaaConnect is the obvious starting point given export documentation and GST refund complexity — this is not a good first candidate for full agent ownership.

## 7. Team & Workload

**Founder personally**
Strategy, legal/compliance decisions, supplier negotiation, pricing, buyer relationships in the early phase, and building the automation layer. Available capacity ~20–40% of time — this is a real constraint and the business must be designed inside it.

**Co-founder**
Species selection, quality assessment, husbandry judgement, supplier vetting, welfare standards. All animal-welfare and species-suitability decisions stay human.

**What AI agents should own**
- Supplier stocklist parsing — dealers send unstructured WhatsApp lists; convert to clean SKUs with verified scientific names. High-frequency, well-defined, ideal fit.
- US buyer prospecting and outreach sequencing — highest-value use, since market access is the actual bottleneck.
- Export documentation drafting — pro-forma invoices, packing lists, certificate paperwork.
- Compliance flagging — species name in, "is this CITES / Schedule IV / US injurious list" out, **as a draft for human verification only, never as a decision**.
- Buyer follow-up, quote tracking, shipment status comms.

**What AI must NOT own**
Species legality determinations, welfare decisions, pricing commitments, anything binding the company legally, and any customer-facing claim about provenance, health status or certification.

**Build sequence**
Build the AI layer after shipment three, not before shipment one. AI multiplies a margin; it cannot create demand. With zero deal flow, every rupee spent on agent infrastructure returns zero.

**Cost of the AI layer**
~₹60–90k hardware (local machine for orchestration), ₹10–20k/month tooling and APIs, 80–150 hours of founder time. Roughly ₹2–3.5L in year one. Expected saving once flow exists: 40–60 hours/month of admin. Payback 6–10 months — but only once shipments are recurring.

**Cost ceiling**
[DECIDED — see decisions.md] ₹10,000–30,000 cap for the validation phase, ₹5,000 approval gate per transaction.

## 8. Risks & Open Questions

**Biggest risk**
No differentiated reason for a US buyer to switch from established South Asian supply lines. Everything else is solvable; this one is existential.

**Second biggest**
Brokerage margin decay. At 10% we are taking half the exporter's profit, and both parties can remove us once they've met. The model has to convert to exporter-of-record before that happens.

**Third**
Compliance exposure. A single mis-declared species creates Lacey Act liability in the US and Wild Life Protection Act liability in India. Under the 2022 amendment, the burden of proof for legal possession sits on the holder. Our brand and our documentation are the product — which means our brand carries the risk too.

**Biggest unknown**
Whether any US buyer will transact with us at all. Unanswerable from a desk. Twenty phone calls answers it.

**Want a second opinion on**
- Broker vs exporter-of-record — and how fast to convert
- Whether this belongs under SevaaConnect or a separate entity — **decided: separate**, see decisions.md
- Whether the aquarium/habitat design studio is a better use of the same founder hours (it has no wildlife-permit exposure, sits directly on the founder's architecture background, generates recurring maintenance revenue, and reaches ₹1L/month on 2–3 clients — it was deferred in the original dossier, and that deferral deserves re-examination)
